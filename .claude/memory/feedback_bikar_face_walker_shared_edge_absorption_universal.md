When bikar's DSL `divide CN into N` runs on a construction circle, the renderer/face-extractor emits all N arc segments around CN as POTENTIAL edges in the face graph — but their actual enrollment as face boundaries is **CONDITIONAL on reachability from a closed traversal cycle through CN's named points**, not unconditional. **Refined 2026-05-26 from Tick 25 falsification** (`arc-cap-and-scalene-triangle`): a 2-circle + chord + polygon construction with `divide C0 into 4` + chord `C0.cpt0 -> C0.cpt1` was expected to produce a 2-sided chord+arc cap face — instead the face-walker produced only 3 shapes (triangle + 2 circles) and the cap faces never materialized. Cross-checked against the corpus baseline `scalene-and-asymmetric-lens` which has `divide C0 into 7` AND emits 5 shapes (lens + triangle + 2 circles + 1 outer) — no cap faces from the 7 divide arcs there either. **Implication:** divide arcs only participate as face boundaries when there's a *closed cycle* of face-graph edges that incorporates them via the divide's named points; a single straight chord between two named points on the circle is NOT sufficient to "close" any cap face. The chord+arc 2-sided cap shape category cannot be witnessed via `divide` + `connect <chord>` alone — it requires either (a) DSL extension for arc-bearing primitives that emit caps explicitly, or (b) explicit named-vertex arcs from `intersect` + `connect arc` pairs forming the cap cycle. If CN intersects any other circle CM used elsewhere in the same scene (CM may be the construction circle of a different polygon, a lens circle CLx, or any circle the pattern names), the CN-CM intersection points become DCEL vertices, and the CN arc segments adjacent to those vertices participate as face-graph edges. The consequence: those arc segments **cut through any face whose interior the CN arcs cross**, either splitting the face into pieces or annexing extra vertices that change the face's `sides` count and silently drop it from `classify .name where sides == N` matches.

**Why:** qiyas#132 Tick 21 (scalene-square-pent-lens-mixed, 2026-05-26) was shipped as falsified with the WRONG mechanism in memory entry name `bikar-face-walker-shared-edge-absorption-universal` (commit 239cff0). The shipped diagnosis claimed a "universal face-walker shared-edge absorption" mechanism unifying Ticks 9/17/20/21. The diagnosis was contradicted by the prior `mixed-topology-edge-and-vertex` Tier 1 entry which ships successfully with the IDENTICAL shared-edge cross-ref pattern (triangle face `source_primitives = {layer:0×3, scalene_tri_poly×3, square_poly×1}`) AND emits both the square and triangle as distinct faces. The real differentiator is the C3 circle: in Tick 21, the pentagon's construction circle C3=(-70,-100) r=70 is `divide`d into 15 segments AND intersects C0 (the square's construction circle) at d=122.07 < sum_r=170, creating a stray DCEL intersection at (-93.96, -34.23) inside the square. The C3 arcs adjacent to that stray cross the square's interior — the square face is split/destroyed. The pentagon itself goes `type=unknown` with `irregular_pent_poly × 6` (instead of expected 5) because it annexed the same stray vertex. The lens face inherits `C3:arc:#0` and `C3:arc:#1` as source primitives — direct evidence that C3 arcs (NEVER referenced by any `connect arc` statement, ONLY emitted by `divide C3 into 15`) participate as face boundaries.

In mixed-topology-edge-and-vertex (the working comparison): C0-C2 d=250 > sum_r=180 (DISJOINT); the explicit divide-circle disjointness check in the template's pre-author geometry block paid off — no construction-circle intersection, no arc-cut, both polygons survive.

**How to apply:** when authoring any Tier 1+ corpus entry that uses `divide CN into N`, **every stray intersection produced by CN's overlap with any other named circle must lie OUTSIDE every face the contract expects to survive**. The bug is not pairwise circle intersection per se — `scalene-and-lens-shared-vertex` (in the corpus) divides C0 into 7 AND C0 intersects CL1 at a stray point, but the stray happens to land outside both the triangle and the lens, so it's benign. The bug fires when the stray lands INSIDE a polygon face — the divide-arc segments adjacent to the stray then cut through the polygon's interior. Concretely:
- **(a) Audit every C-C intersection in the template's pre-author geometry block.** For each pair of named circles, compute `d = dist(centers)`. If `|r1-r2| < d < r1+r2`, they intersect at 2 points — compute both points explicitly. Any intersection point that is NOT a shared vertex referenced in some `polygon` or `connect arc` statement is a "stray." For each stray, check whether it lies inside any polygon's bounding region; if yes, the contract is violated.
- **(b) Lens construction (`intersect Xn Ca Cb` + `connect arc` pairs) on `divide`-less circles is by-design intersecting and safe.** The lens circles' arcs only participate where explicitly named.
- **(c) If a construction circle MUST intersect another circle AND would land a stray inside a polygon face, DO NOT call `divide` on either circle at the conflicting `into N`.** Use a one-off intersection (`intersect XX CN CM` then reference `XX.cpt0`) for the named vertex, leaving the rest of CN's arcs unenrolled.
- **(d) Easier conservative rule (use when in doubt):** keep all `divide`d circles pairwise disjoint (`d > r1+r2`) or one strictly inside the other (`d < |r1-r2|`). This sidesteps the stray-inside-polygon failure mode entirely.
- **(e) BOUNDARY-PASSING-THROUGH-INTERIOR rule (added 2026-05-26 from Tick 22 falsification):** even with NO stray intersection (e.g., tangent at a named shared vertex), if a `divide`d circle's BOUNDARY (any arc segment) passes through ANOTHER polygon face's interior, that polygon face gets cut by the divide arcs. Tick 22 falsified the quintuple-junction-shared-vertex attempt: C4=(140,0,40) was ext-tangent to C0 at the named junction (100,0), so no stray intersection existed — yet C4's disk overlapped the irregular_quad's interior (the quad spans x=100 to x=310 along y=0, and C4 spans x=100 to x=180 around y=0), and C4's 28 arc segments sliced the quad into pieces. Result: 14 emitted shapes including type=unknown polyclass faces with cross-contaminated source_primitives (irregular_hept_poly tags bleeding into quad/pent/tri faces). **The full rule:** no `divide`d circle's disk (bounded region) may overlap any other polygon face's interior region. Tangency at a single point is NOT sufficient — the test must verify whether the divide-circle's open disk has empty intersection with each polygon's interior. This rule applies independently of (a)-(d); even when (d) holds, (e) can still fire.

Do NOT iterate further "stray-intersection-inside-polygon" Tier 1 entries — Tick 21 is the witness; any new entry repeating this construction with a stray landing inside a polygon face is pre-falsified.

The original (wrong) Tick 21 diagnosis (shipped in 239cff0) — "universal shared-edge absorption unifying Ticks 9/17/20/21" — was contradicted by the working mixed-topology-edge-and-vertex / dual-asymmetric-mixed-topology / tri-topology-three-primitive entries that all ship with the same shared-edge cross-ref pattern. Ticks 9, 17, and 20 remain valid as their own three independent face-extractor traps (per-edge tag polyclass; polygon-edge absorbed by coincident arcs; classify-by-predicate polyclass on same-sides) — but Tick 21 is a FOURTH independent trap with a different mechanism (divide-circle arcs cutting other faces) and the shared-edge story does not apply to it.
